# Verification before automation — 2026-11-17

Cross-sector hypothesis: users may pay for evidence that a change was intentional and safely reversible, not for more automation. Sources: [Trusty CSV](https://www.producthunt.com/products/trusty-csv), [Cookie Away](https://www.producthunt.com/products/cookie-away), [StateWeave](https://www.producthunt.com/products/stateweave), [vatnode](https://vatnode.dev/), [HTPBE](https://htpbe.tech/). These are product/creator statements, not proven retention.

## Test: Change Evidence Kit
Build a local CLI producing CSV/PDF structural diffs and a shareable HTML report in 1–2 weeks. Interview eight independent operators with recent concrete failures; recruit five testers with anonymized files. Success: four concrete incidents, three completed trials, two repeat uses and two explicit paid-integration intentions. Failure: fewer than two repeat users or existing tools solve the job. Do not upload private documents or equate PDF modifications with fraud.

## Counterexamples
Git diff may already satisfy developer users; document formats are hard to compare; PDF structure changes can be benign; self-reported product metrics and public comments are not PMF evidence.
